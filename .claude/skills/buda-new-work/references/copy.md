# The copy

One interview feeds three outputs. Do it once, properly, then write.

## The interview

Ask all of these in one batch, as one message. Short questions, no preamble.

**Problem**
1. Who came to you, and what was actually wrong? Not the deliverable they asked
   for — the thing that was not working.
2. What had they already tried, or what were they afraid of?

**Proposal**
3. What did you propose, and what did you say no to?
4. Why that, and not the obvious version?

**Execution**
5. What did you actually make? Numbers: how many images, how many days, how
   many pieces, how big the crew.
6. What was hard, or what changed halfway?

**Result**
7. What happened. Numbers again if they exist — attendance, reach, sales,
   whether it is still running.
8. What did the client say or do afterwards?

**Attribution**
9. Anyone else's work in here? Design that was already there, a second shooter,
   a Crewcible job?

Two follow-ups worth asking almost every time: *what would have happened if you
had not been there*, and *what is the one image you would show if you only got
one*. The first gives the result its edge, the second picks the cover.

| 8, plus the edge | — | `reflection` | the last line before the links |

The page is the fullest version, the video is the tightest (about 90 words
total), the `.txt` sits between (130–185 words).

## Voice

Benjamin's, not a portfolio's:

- Plain verbs. `wrote`, `shot`, `cut`, `built`. Not *authored*, *utilised*,
  *crafted*, *delivered*.
- Concrete nouns and real numbers. "Nineteen shopfronts" beats "a range of
  local businesses".
- Flat superlatives when they are true. "It was the first" needs no hedge.
- Uneven rhythm. Three paragraphs of identical length read as machine output.
- No em dashes. No promotional adjectives — *seamless*, *robust*,
  *comprehensive*, *vibrant* are all out. No aphoristic sign-off.
- No forced rule of three. Use the number of things that exist.

Run `/no-ai-slop` (or the `humanizer` skill) over every text before it ships.

## The one hard test

**Every reflection must be true of this project and no other.**

That is the whole difference between a portfolio post and a motivational one.

> "Restraint is harder than volume" earns its place on Music ACT because the
> organisation exists to amplify other people.
> "It ran for five years. It has since been replaced with a version I did not
> make" could only be National Triangle.

If a line would survive being pasted under a different job, it is not finished.
Rewrite it, do not soften it.

## Attribution

Say whose work it was when it was not all yours. There is precedent:

- Pat Rose on Angus Comyns and Queensland Neuro.
- "The identity was already there when I arrived" on ConnectUp.
- Crewcible jobs carry their own rules — read
  `memory/project_crewcible_attribution.md` before writing one. Crewcible's
  clients are not Benjamin's clients and the post must not imply otherwise.

Credit costs nothing and a claim that overreaches costs the whole post.

## The `.txt`

Written to `$BL/out/<slug>/<slug>.txt`, pasted next to the video.

Shape, in order:

```
Title
Discipline line

Hook paragraph.

Two or three paragraphs.

A live link, if there is one.

The disciplines, as a plain sentence.
benjaminarnedo.com

#hashtags
```

**The hook has to land in the first line.** Roughly 210 characters show before
LinkedIn's "see more", and nobody expands a post that has not already earned it.
Do not open with the client's name and a comma.

Most of the material is already on the case study page by the time you write
this. Tighten that. Do not invent a new claim that is not on the page.
